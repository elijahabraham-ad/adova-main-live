One forehand, four stages.

This is what a lesson actually fixes. Scroll and the racket moves through the swing. Tap the court to hit it again.

Stage 01 · Ready Tap to swing
01

Ready

Little hop as the ball leaves their strings, weight forward, racket up in front of you. Nothing has happened yet, and that is the point. Most missed forehands are already lost right here, standing flat.

02

Takeback

Shoulder turns first, hands follow. The racket goes back early so it never has to rush forward. Early beats fast, every single time.

03

Contact

Out in front, off the front hip, strings on the outside of the ball. This is the only moment the ball actually feels, so this is the one we drill until it is automatic.

04

Follow-through

Finish up and across your body and let the shoulder do the last of the work. A full finish is what turns a poke into a shot with real shape on it, and it leaves you balanced for the next ball.

That is the whole method: find the one piece costing you the point, fix it, then hit enough balls that you stop thinking about it.

Sheana grew up in New York and was handed a racket young. Her aunt and uncle coached her through most of her junior career, and she went on to play Division I college tennis. Somewhere in there she picked up the thing she teaches now: a kid who is enjoying the work will out-practice a kid who is grinding through it.

The name is not decoration. When a lesson stops being fun, the technique stops sticking.

She teaches math to teenagers by day and coaches them on court after the bell. Through the Atlanta Youth Tennis & Education Foundation at Fair Oaks, she coordinates high school tennis for kids across South Cobb. Enjoyable Tennis is where she takes private students of her own, from five-year-olds swinging at a red ball to adults finally learning a real second serve.
